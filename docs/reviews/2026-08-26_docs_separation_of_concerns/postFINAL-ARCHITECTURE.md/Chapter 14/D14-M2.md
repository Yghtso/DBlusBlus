# D14-M2 architecture integration report

1. **Verdict:** D14-M2 is **RESOLVED AND INTEGRATED**. B14-2 is **CLOSED**.

2. **Initial Git state**

   - Working tree: clean
   - Index: clean
   - HEAD: `fe98d46e78a33ed5469aa7997b8d90d06b82fe47`

3. **D14-M1 preservation:** D14-M1 was already committed at the initial HEAD. Its transaction classification, registration, cutoff clamp, C5/A3 release, and snapshot separation remain byte-for-byte unchanged. No adjacent D14-M1 synchronization was required.

4. **Review artifact preservation:** The previously reported untracked B14.1 artifact was absent at task start and remains absent. No review artifact was read, created, or modified.

5. **Original B14-2 failure:** `txn_status_reclaim_before=C` could become durable while the only status-independent tuple/catalog state existed in volatile dirty frames or undurable WAL.

6. **Final invariant:** A durably authoritative cutoff `C` guarantees that every persistent dependency on every normal `T<C` is either already durable in status-independent form or reconstructable before READY from durable retained WAL.

## Recovery-safety proof

7. **Crash-recoverable status independence:** After any crash at or after durable cutoff publication, recovery can reach READY without requiring a terminal outcome below `C`.

8. **Durable-page proof:** The resulting status-independent page state is durably stored through the ordinary stable-image writeback rules in §§7.10 and 12.17.

9. **Durable-WAL proof:** The authorizing/reconstructive WAL is durable and remains retained until the corresponding page no longer depends on it.

10. **Retention requirement:** WAL durability covers the immediate crash boundary; DPT/`rec_lsn` and installed-checkpoint retention prevent later recycling, including before a second crash.

11. **Checkpoint requirement:** A checkpoint is not mandatory. It may contribute to an ordinary durable-page or retention proof.

12. **Data-page force:** Not mandatory. Dirty pages may remain NO-FORCE when durable retained WAL is sufficient.

13. **Required WAL target:**

```text
required_wal_target =
    maximum authorizing record-start LSN
    among WAL-dependent prerequisite mutations
```

If the set is nonempty, `durable_lsn >= required_wal_target` must hold before control publication.

14. **`durable_lsn`:** Its existing complete-record-prefix semantics remain unchanged. No end-LSN or OS byte-position domain was introduced.

15. **Control versus WAL synchronization:** `fdatasync(database.control)` does not make unrelated WAL durable. Required WAL durability must be established first.

## Publication ordering

16. **Canonical order**

   1. Establish candidate `C` under status-history coordination.
   2. Exclude D14-M1 future-publication blockers and active guards.
   3. Complete and revalidate eligible freeze, normalization, removal, or other status-independence actions.
   4. Classify each prerequisite state as durable-page or WAL-dependent.
   5. Establish WAL durability through the required target.
   6. Confirm DPT/checkpoint retention preserves all reconstruction sources.
   7. Publish and synchronize the next control slot under §13.2.4.
   8. Publish the matching runtime cutoff.
   9. Retire BufferPool/DPT status-page ownership and optionally punch sparse ranges.

17. **Runtime cutoff:** Still published only after durable control publication.

18. **Physical status-page reclamation:** Still occurs only after the cutoff is authoritative and runtime pins/I/O/frame ownership permit retirement.

## Prerequisite mutations

19. **Creator freezing:** Eligibility and visibility semantics are unchanged. A freeze authorizes cutoff advancement only when its page is durable or its reconstructive WAL is durable and retained.

20. **Aborted-`xmax` normalization:** The ABORTED proof and rewrite are unchanged; the resulting page has the same durability requirement.

21. **Physical removal:** Removal used as status-independence proof must itself be durable or WAL-reconstructable. DEAD/in-memory classification alone is insufficient. RID/index barriers are unchanged.

## Crash outcomes

22. **Crash matrix**

| Boundary | Result |
|---|---|
| Freeze/normalization authorized, WAL undurable, page dirty, control old | `C` cannot publish; old cutoff and old status history remain authoritative |
| WAL durable and retained, page dirty, control old | Crash selects old cutoff; replayed extra normalization/freezing is harmless |
| Control `C` durable, old page on disk, WAL retained | Recovery replays the page mutation before READY |
| Cutoff durable, page remains dirty after recovery | DPT/checkpoint retention preserves WAL through a later crash |
| Cutoff durable, sparse status-page reclamation fails | `C` remains authoritative; physical status storage may remain |

23. **Old-control fallback:** No status history required by the old cutoff may be retired before durable `C`. A known failed publication retains the old authority; uncertain/torn publication is resolved deterministically on reopen.

24. **New-control result:** If the complete new slot is selected, recovery may rely on `C` because its recovery-safety prerequisites preceded the publication attempt.

25. **Forbidden state:** `C` durable + status-dependent old disk page + missing required reconstruction WAL is architecturally impossible.

## Recovery, retention, and checkpoint relationships

26. **DPT/`rec_lsn`:** Cutoff publication does not clear or reset an ordinary dirty-page recovery dependency.

27. **TXN_STATUS `F`:** Preparatory `F=rec_lsn` and later required terminal records remain retained until the dependency clears or the wholly retired page is safely removed from BufferPool/DPT ownership.

28. **WAL recycling:** Cannot discard reconstruction WAL for a still-dependent dirty heap, catalog, system, or status page.

29. **Checkpoint:** Either checkpoint-before-cutoff or cutoff-before-checkpoint is legal. The selected checkpoint/control generations must preserve all reconstruction sources. No checkpoint format changed.

## Failure behavior

30. **WAL flush failure:** Prevents new cutoff publication. Already-authorized page progress remains ordinary maintenance progress.

31. **WAL uncertainty:** Uses existing §12.12 noncontinuable/no-guessing semantics. No cutoff is published.

32. **Control publication failure:** A known nonpublication leaves the old cutoff authoritative. Under uncertain physical outcome, reopen may select either the old slot or a complete valid new slot; both are safe.

33. **Status-page deletion failure:** Leaves safe extra storage. A successful durable cutoff is not rolled back.
