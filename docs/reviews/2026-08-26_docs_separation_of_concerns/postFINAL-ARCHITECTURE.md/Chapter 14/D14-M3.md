## Claim and handoff semantics

7. A live `TUPLE_WRITE` request is a runtime physical RID-retention claim for its exact `(TableId,RID)` key. It is not a visibility, status-history, or persistent-format claim.

8. Every queued request that can still receive a grant counts, regardless of queue position.

9. Every granted owner counts through ordinary terminal lock release.

10. The discovery read epoch remains active until the `TUPLE_WRITE` request is registered in canonical LockManager state. Only then may the epoch be released.

11. Immediate grant overlaps the read epoch: granted ownership exists before epoch release.

12. Blocking wait:

   - register queued request while epoch-protected;
   - release epoch;
   - block;
   - obtain grant;
   - re-fetch and revalidate.

13. Request registration and RID reuse now have one gap-free ordering:

   - registration first: the request blocks reuse;
   - reuse first: epoch-protected revalidation prevents admission of an old-identity request.

14. If registration fails before the request becomes live, the caller still holds its read epoch and must fail or retry. It cannot release the epoch and wait or continue unprotected.

## Claim lifetime

15. A waiter remains a claim for as long as it can still receive a grant.

16. Cancellation/removal and loss of grant eligibility are one semantic event. A removed request cannot later receive a grant or resume ownership.

17. For deadlock victims:

   - a queued claim ends only upon canonical removal that makes grant impossible;
   - a granted claim remains through ordinary abort cleanup at A3.

18. Granted-owner lifetime:

   - COMMIT: retained through C4, released during C5;
   - ABORT: retained through A2, released during A3.

19. Post-wait re-fetch and revalidation remain mandatory.

## RID reuse result

21. Same-RID reuse now requires:

   - global MVCC reclamation eligibility;
   - required index cleanup;

   - predecessor-link splicing/proof;
   - exact read-epoch grace;
   - zero queued or granted `TUPLE_WRITE` claims;
   - applicable page/slot/frame prerequisites;
   - canonical `DEAD -> UNUSED`;
   - only then allocation as a new `NORMAL` identity.

22. A live claim blocks `DEAD -> UNUSED` and same-RID rebinding. It does not necessarily block eligible `NORMAL -> DEAD`, index cleanup, freezing, or normalization.
